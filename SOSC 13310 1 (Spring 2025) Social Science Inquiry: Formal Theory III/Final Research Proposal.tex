\section{Motivation and Introduction}
In each period, a crime against a dictator will occur. The following game happens in each period:
\begin{itemize}
\item Dictator $D$ has chance to punish a crime, $p^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}.$ 
\item If $p^{(t)} = 1,$ then the crime will be martyred and everyone rebels the dictator is overthrown with probability $1-\theta.$ If $p^{(t)} = 0,$ people in the population $i \in [0,1]$ can choose to rebel $a_i = 1$ or not  $a_i = 0$ in a global game after seeing this punishment. The payoffs for each individual are given by  \begin{table}[H]
            \centering
        \begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
             & Win & Lose\\
             \hline
             $a_i = 1$ & $r -c$ & $-c$ \\
             \hline
             $a_i = 0$ &  $r$& $0$\\
        \end{tabular}
        \caption{}
    \end{table}

\item When $p = 0,$ The rebellion succeeds iff 
\[\int_0^1 a_i di \geq \theta,\] where $\theta \in [0,1]$ is the strength of the dictator. $\theta$ is private to the dictator, but has a uniform prior $\theta \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1).$
People have a belief
\[\bbP\{\theta \mid \mathcal{F}_t\} =:\mu_t(\theta)\]

Each person in the population will receive a signal about the strength of the dictator, 
\[s_i^{(t)} = \theta + \varepsilon_i^{(t)},\] where $\varepsilon_i^{(1)}$ is drawn from some cdf $F_{(t)}.$ We have showed in class that the rebellion will succeed if and only if 
\[\theta \leq 1 - \frac{c}{r} = \theta*_{(1)}\] and soldiers will fight if and only if 
\[s_i \leq = k^{(1)} = 1 - \frac{c}{r} + F_{(1)}(1 - \frac{c}{r})\]
\item The villagers receive better signals about the weakness of $D$ the more the dictator decides to spare the crime. That is, the more $D$ does $p_k = 0,$ the lower $s_i^{(t)}$ will be.
\end{itemize}
